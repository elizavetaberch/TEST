### ECONOMY

Many people aren't familiar with the specific tactics of the eco strategy.

Everyone is used to two main economic strategies. Either a player takes 9 mechs and pushes with 3 factories until the end of the game, or they go Iron/Awasy, greedily grabbing every available mech and unlocking their economic potential for the late game.

But the economy in the game is very flexible.

Just as an example, I'll show you an intermediate build that, when executed properly, can outperform both of these economic concepts.

This works for any faction; here we're talking strictly about the economic model.

We take 10–14 mechs. We don't build 3 factories right from the start; we build 1–2 at the beginning. 14 mechs maximum.

We don't invest heavily in the army at the start, but we can still fend off any 3–9-mech rush. We can afford turrets and shields. And we have a decent amount of spam to fend off ground attacks and prevent our side from being overrun. After securing the mechs, we pour everything—absolutely everything—into the army: just the factories, the minimum amount of energy needed to maintain those factories, stars if you like, and we just push forward. We'll crush any 9-mech start. And we'll take down an eco player who goes for a full eco build. Because their factories simply won't be up in time, and their army won't stand a chance against our fully developed industry. It's hard to call this a timing attack. It's a sort of hybrid eco strategy.

General advice: Both of these concepts. These aren't just strategies you should follow to the letter and use consistently from game to game.

Play your standard build and one or two of these concepts alternately, depending on who you're playing against and which map you're on. This playstyle and these micro/macro strategies add variety to your gameplay and are designed to make life difficult for your opponent.

Hide what you're doing during the game, deceive and confuse your opponent, and combine these tactics. Play to your opponent's strengths and buy yourself time. Impose your own rules and draw your opponent into situations where you're comfortable playing. As soon as your opponent makes a mistake, you must punish them; as soon as they start losing ground, apply pressure and don't let up—seize the initiative and hold onto it.

And learn to play a deep late game. You must be strong at every stage of the game. Reach a level of play where you're difficult to counter at any stage. In the late game, our tanks become so heavily armored and powerful that even a bomber can't destroy them with a single charge. If you can maintain your economy and military production, you're guaranteed to crush your opponent; the longer the tech race goes on, the weaker the demolition and any slash damage become. Focus on more splits and map control, and you'll be unstoppable.
